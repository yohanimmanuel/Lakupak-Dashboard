Many digital marketplaces optimize for **maximum listing overlap** and **lowest displayed price**. That model works for some consumer categories. It often harms **wholesale distributors** who compete on service, credit, assortment, and territory as much as on unit price.

Lakupak takes a **controlled marketplace** approach instead.

## Distributor stays in charge

Each distributor on Lakupak runs an isolated storefront:

- Only **published** products are visible to buyers
- **Pricing** and **offers** are set by the distributor, not by the platform auctioning leads
- **Buyer relationships** belong to the distributor; Lakupak records history to serve them better
- **Shipping and payment** rules reflect how that distributor already works

Buyers discover and order digitally. The commercial relationship remains **B2B and bilateral**.

## What buyers see (and do not see)

| Buyers can | Buyers typically cannot |
|------------|-------------------------|
| Shop catalogs from distributors they access | Compare the same SKU across unrelated distributors in one race-to-bottom grid |
| Follow distributors they trust | See other distributors' confidential buyer lists |
| Use offers the distributor activates | Force a distributor to honor a platform-wide flash sale they never approved |

This reduces unhealthy **price wars** and protects territories sales teams have built over years.

## Why this matters in Indonesia

Warungs and kiosks often buy from **one primary wholesaler** per category, plus occasional top-ups. Trust, credit, and delivery reliability matter as much as a few rupiah on a carton.

A controlled model matches how trade actually works:

- The warung reorders from **their** distributor
- The distributor competes on **service**, not only on being the cheapest row in a public table
- The platform makes repeat orders **easier**, not anonymous

## Operating platform plus marketplace

Lakupak combines:

1. **Private operations** (Dashboard): stock, CRM, catalog, orders, offers
2. **Buyer-facing storefront** (Marketplace): modern ordering for authorised retailers

Distributors can use the operations side even as they grow marketplace adoption with their existing buyer base.
